## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Copy-specific but composition-generic. The words are genuinely grounded in the business (grinds, Junior Cycle, Leaving Cert O/H, 7 H1s, the sailing/windsurfing anecdote), but the visual system is a template seen everywhere: cream background, eyebrow-label-over-heading pattern, three-card grid, four-step process, two-tier pricing with a "featured" badge, monogram-circle avatar, navy CTA band. Two concrete tells beyond vibe: Fraunces is one of the most recognizable "AI-generated site" serif choices, and the CSS custom properties are semantically wrong for their own values (`--blue:#bf4d28` is burnt orange/rust, `--green:#c98a26` is gold/ochre, `--navy:#20443f` is dark teal) — a strong signal the palette was inherited from a template rather than built for this brand.

**Deterministic scan**: 6 findings, all `overused-font` (Fraunces), at lines 9, 34, 97, 126, 141, 172. This directly confirms the LLM's specificity concern with exact locations — every one of the 6 hits is a Fraunces declaration (the Google Fonts import plus 5 CSS rules). No other rule categories fired; no false positives to flag.

**Visual overlays**: Not available this run — no browser automation tool was exposed in this session, so no live `[Human]`-tab overlay could be injected. This is a tooling gap, not a finding about the page.

## Overall Impression

The copy and content strategy are genuinely well done — real Irish schooling vocabulary, an honest no-fabricated-proof stance, and a human, specific "About" story. But the visual execution sitting on top of that content is an interchangeable template, and two of the page's actual interactive mechanics (mobile nav, WhatsApp link) silently depend on JavaScript succeeding with no fallback — for a site whose entire job is getting a nervous student or parent to make first contact, a broken mobile menu or a dead WhatsApp button is the single biggest risk on the page.

## What's Working

1. **The sailing/windsurfing anecdote** in the About section — a specific, real, humanizing detail no competitor could copy-paste. The single best piece of brand-specific writing on the page.
2. **Locally accurate terminology** throughout ("grinds," Junior Cycle, Leaving Cert Ordinary & Higher, H1s) — no translation tax for the actual Irish audience.
3. **Dual low-friction contact**, executed twice (prefilled mailto + WhatsApp deep link), directly serving the "frictionless first contact" goal from the product brief.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] WhatsApp CTA is a dead link without JS**
- **Why it matters**: `<a href="#" id="whatsappLink">` only gets its real `https://wa.me/...` URL set by the closing `<script>` block. Any JS error earlier in that same script (it's one block — an exception on one line stops the rest) leaves one of the page's two conversion channels silently pointing at `#`. Directly undermines the core business goal.
- **Fix**: Hardcode the working `https://wa.me/353864002436?text=...` URL as the static `href` in HTML; use JS only to enhance (e.g. update the prefilled text), never to construct base functionality.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

**[P0] Mobile nav is unusable without JS**
- **Why it matters**: `.nav-links{display:none}` below 800px is toggled solely by a click handler with no CSS-only fallback (`<details>/<summary>` or checkbox hack). If JS doesn't run, mobile visitors — plausibly the majority of first-time visitors — can't reach Subjects, Formats, About, or "Book a grind" at all.
- **Fix**: Add a no-JS-safe pattern, or at minimum reflect open/closed state via `aria-expanded` and don't rely on JS alone for something this load-bearing.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Overused, template-signaling typeface (Fraunces)**
- **Why it matters**: Confirmed by both the design review and the detector (6 hits, lines 9/34/97/126/141/172). Fraunces is one of the most common "AI-generated site" serif choices, reinforcing the generic-template read and undercutting the credibility-through-specificity positioning from the product brief.
- **Fix**: Swap the display serif for something less ubiquitous that still reads warm/academic; confirmed with you as open to exploring this.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable typeset`

**[P1] CSS color tokens are misnamed relative to their actual hues**
- **Why it matters**: `--blue` is burnt orange, `--green` is gold, `--navy` is dark teal. Beyond being confusing for future edits, it's evidence the palette was inherited from a template rather than chosen deliberately for a maths/physics tutoring brand.
- **Fix**: Rename tokens to match reality, or better, revisit whether this exact warm-editorial palette is the right one for this brand.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable colorize`

**[P1] No real photo of Luke**
- **Why it matters**: The About section's avatar is a generic gradient-circle-with-a-letter — visually identical to any SaaS placeholder. The product brief states personal credibility from a named, real tutor is the entire differentiator versus an agency; hiding the actual face of the person a parent is trusting with live 1-1 sessions works against that.
- **Fix**: Replace with a real photo (with alt text) once available.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable adapt`

**[P2] Pricing section offers no anchor and thin reassurance**
- **Why it matters**: The only pricing copy on the page is "get in touch for exact pricing." Allowed by the no-fixed-pricing constraint, but nothing compensates for the anxiety that gap creates for a cost-conscious parent right before they'd need to email a stranger.
- **Fix**: Add one explicit reassurance line inside the format cards (e.g. "No obligation — pricing is discussed openly on your free intro chat, before anything is booked").
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer)**: The hero stat uses "JC – Undergrad" as the very first mention of levels on the page — before "Junior Cycle" is ever spelled out (that happens later, in Subjects). A parent unfamiliar with Irish schooling shorthand hits an opaque acronym first. Also, the two format cards (1-1 vs Group) list features but never state relative cost or a "choose this if..." rule — the product brief calls for this decision to "never be ambiguous," but Jordan must email before learning anything about cost.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**: The hero headline is still 3 lines at the mobile breakpoint (2.1rem), eating a lot of vertical space before any CTA is reached one-handed. The hamburger button also gives zero tap feedback beyond the menu appearing — no `aria-expanded`, no icon change — so a fast, distracted tap has no confirmation it registered.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent)**: Decorative glyphs in the subject-card icons (ƒ, ∑, ⚡) and the repeated checkmark (✓) across roughly a dozen list items have no `aria-hidden="true"` — a screen reader will announce "function," "n-ary summation," "high voltage sign," and "check mark" before nearly every line of content. No custom `:focus-visible` styling is defined anywhere despite heavily restyled buttons/links, so keyboard focus visibility on the core CTAs is unverified against the sticky, blurred header.
